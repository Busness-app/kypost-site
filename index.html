Privacy by default

End-to-end means your server can't read it either

KyPost now lets your private key live in your browser instead of on the server — and everywhere encryption would quietly weaken, it stops and asks first.

01 — Key custody

Your key, or your server's

Choose client-held keys and your private key is wrapped under your password and unwrapped only in your browser — the server can't decrypt your mail, and it no longer caches decrypted bodies to disk. Choose server-held and you keep the convenience, honestly labeled.

02 — Finding keys

Encryption without the key-signing party

Your server looks recipients up through Web Key Directory, then keyservers, and harvests Autocrypt headers from DKIM-authenticated mail — pinning what it finds on first use. Each rung is yours to switch off, any discovered key is yours to reject, and scanning a fingerprint in person is still there when it matters.

03 — Publishing

Be findable on your own domain

Verify a domain once by DNS, and your server serves WKD for it — so anyone in Thunderbird or GnuPG can encrypt to you unprompted. Publication is opt-out per user and only ever for addresses you actually send from.

04 — No silent downgrade

It refuses rather than weakens

An encrypted send to someone with no key on file is refused, not quietly turned into something weaker. Confirm the fallback and you get a one-time pickup link — sealed in your own browser, so your server stores only ciphertext it can't open.

05 — Metadata

The subject line is inside the envelope

Encrypted mail carries its real subject inside the encrypted part using the LAMPS protected-headers convention, so the outer envelope says nothing. Push notifications are generic by default — sender and subject only travel to Google or Apple if you turn previews on.

06 — Under the hood

Your server, your rules

Mail lives on infrastructure you control, and unread-mail classification runs on a local Ollama model — no cloud AI provider ever sees your inbox.

Hardened by design

Encryption is the start, not the finish

Beyond PGP, every client now ships device-level protections — because a compromised device, or a convincing email, shouldn't mean a compromised inbox.

App Lock

An unlock gates the app before your inbox ever opens — a PIN with escalating delays and wipe-on-repeat on Android and Linux, Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode on Apple platforms.

Hostile Location Protection

A one-tap mode that keeps zero on-device cache of mail, contacts, or attachments — built for border crossings and other moments where your device itself is the risk.

Pinned server certificate

Your server's certificate is pinned the moment you pair and the check fails closed after that, so a network that turns hostile later can't quietly swap in a fake one.

Credentials behind the lock

Your relay credential can be sealed behind your PIN, so background mail checks and MFA approvals wait until you actually unlock the app rather than running on their own.

Phishing that wears our face

Every client registers the kypost:// link scheme, so a link in an email could once raise a real pairing prompt pointing at an attacker's server. Mail impersonating KyPost is now flagged on the server, and the clients refuse the scheme outright.

Sandboxed message rendering

Sender HTML renders with JavaScript off, remote content blocked, and navigation out of the message denied — and a blocked link is labeled as blocked instead of silently doing nothing.

No backups, no extraction

Device backup and data-extraction are disabled at the OS level, so nothing here ends up in a cloud backup or a forensic pull.

Screen-capture protection

Mail, PGP, and security screens are excluded from recordings and screen sharing — as far as each OS actually allows, which is documented rather than overstated.

These protections have landed on Android, Mac & iOS, and Linux, each using the strongest primitive its platform offers — so the details differ where the OS does.

Make it yours

Categories, rules, and 15 themes — shared everywhere

Every client speaks the same theme language, so the way you sort mail and the way it looks travels with you.

Categories

Unread mail is auto-labeled by a local AI model and sorted into keyword tabs — no manual filing.

Rules

Tune the classifier's prompt per user to change what counts as which category, right from the web UI.

Themes

15 palettes, from Patina Ky to Cyber Punk, defined once and shared byte-for-byte across web, Android, Mac, iOS, and Linux.

Filtering

Inbox rules run as an expanded Sieve script on your own server — write a condition once and every client sees the same filtered result.

Notifications

Pick your delivery path per device: Firebase, APNs, or UnifiedPush for instant push, or plain client pull if you'd rather trade a little immediacy for a lot more privacy. Either way the alert is generic unless you opt into previews.

Contacts

Manage your contacts from any client, with full synchronization across web, Android, Mac, iOS, and Linux.
